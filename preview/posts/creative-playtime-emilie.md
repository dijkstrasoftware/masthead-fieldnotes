I met her on a grey Tuesday, the kind of light that makes a studio look like a painting. Coffee in mismatched cups, a radio somewhere, a workbench covered in things half-finished.

**How did the studio start?**

Honestly, by accident. I was making pieces for friends, then friends of friends, and one day the kitchen table wasn't big enough anymore.

> Small is a choice. It lets me say no to the things that don't feel like mine.

**What does a good day look like?**

Slow mornings, no email until noon, and at least one thing I didn't plan to make. The best work comes out of the afternoons I'd written off.

## On running it alone

It's lonely sometimes. But it's also the first time the work has felt entirely mine — every mistake, every small win.

She walks me out past the drying rack. On the way, she hands me a ring that didn't make the cut. "It's not wrong," she says. "It's just not finished being itself yet."
